A basement bath is one of the most common ways Denver owners add space without an addition: a guest bath for visiting family, a bathroom for a teenager's room, or a full bath that turns a finished basement into a real suite. It is also the bathroom project with the most decisions hidden under the floor. This page walks through them in the order they usually come up.

What permits does a basement bathroom need in Denver?

Denver Community Planning and Development lets simple trade work, such as replacing a shower, a toilet, a GFCI outlet or an exhaust fan, go through quick permits with no plan review. A new bathroom is different. CPD says most projects cannot be quick permits, including structural work, and a basement bath almost always involves new walls, new drain lines and new circuits. Expect to apply for a building permit with plan review through Denver's e-permits system, then line up the trades under it.

Can the basement drain reach the sewer by gravity?

Everything in a bathroom drains downhill, so the first question is whether there is any "down" left. If the main sewer line leaves your house below the basement floor, a plumber can usually trench the slab and run new drains to it with the slope they need. If the sewer exits through the foundation wall above the floor, or the new bathroom sits far from the main line, the fixtures sit lower than the pipe they need to reach. Then the water has to be pumped up.

Why test for radon before you finish the basement?

Colorado's geology gives off radon, a colorless, odorless gas that seeps into houses through the slab. A March 2026 article from the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus put it plainly: about 50% of Colorado homes tested for radon are above the recommended threshold, and as of 2025 about 500 people a year in the state die of lung cancer caused by radon exposure. The EPA's action level is 4 picocuries per liter (pCi/L).

A basement bathroom changes the math in two ways. People start spending real time on the lowest level, and the EPA says that when you begin occupying a lower level of your home, you should retest on that level. And a mitigation system is far easier to route before the walls close: the suction pipe and fan path can be planned with the framing instead of cut through it later.

  • Test first. Place the kit in the lowest lived-in area. CU Anschutz notes free kits are available from several sources, including its cancer center.
  • At or above 4 pCi/L? Get a written mitigation plan from a qualified radon contractor before framing. Colorado's health department runs a low-income radon mitigation assistance program for eligible households.
  • Retest after the remodel and after any mitigation, so you know the finished space is where you want it.

What else makes a basement bathroom work long term?

  • A fan that actually leaves the house. Basements already run damp. Duct the exhaust fan to the outdoors, not into the joist space, and size it for the room.
  • Materials that tolerate moisture. Cement board or a waterproofing membrane behind tile, and flooring that can handle a wet day.
  • A one-piece shower base. On a slab, a molded shower pan or kit avoids a site-built mortar bed and many of the leak points that come with it. Showers4Less carries shower packages with matched bases and walls that contractors install in basements.
  • Think about who will use it in 10 years. A basement bath is handy for guests, but stairs are a barrier for anyone with knee, hip or balance trouble. If the bathroom is meant for an aging parent, a low-step shower on the main floor is usually the better investment.

Denver basement bathroom FAQ

Do I need a permit to add a basement bathroom in Denver?

Yes, in almost every case. A new bathroom involves new walls, drains and circuits, and Denver says structural work cannot use a quick permit. Expect a building permit with plan review plus plumbing, electrical and mechanical permits.

Do I need a pump for a basement bathroom?

Only if the fixtures sit below the sewer line or too far from it to slope downhill. If the main sewer leaves the house below the slab, a gravity drain is often possible. If it exits through the wall above the floor, plan on an ejector basin or an up-flush system.

Should I test for radon before finishing a basement in Colorado?

Yes. About half of Colorado homes tested are above the EPA action level of 4 pCi/L, and the EPA says to retest when you start using a lower level. Testing before framing lets a mitigation system be planned into the walls.

Can I pull my own permit for a basement bathroom in Denver?

Only if it is a single-family house that you own and occupy. The trade portions require passing Denver's homeowner exam, and you agree to keep living there for a year after the job. In a condo, townhome or duplex, a licensed contractor has to pull it.

What kind of shower works best in a basement?

A one-piece molded shower base or a shower kit with matched walls is the simplest on a concrete slab, because it avoids a site-built mortar bed. The drain location still has to suit the gravity line or pump system.
