Denver bathroom remodel permits, without the guesswork

Quick permit or plan review? What Denver's own pages say.

Short answer: most Denver bathroom remodels need at least one permit. Replacing a shower, tub, sink, toilet, water heater or exhaust fan can usually go through a quick permit, which Denver issues without plan review. Moving walls or other structural changes need a building permit with plan review. Homeowners can pull their own permits only for a single-family home they live in.

Denver Community Planning and Development (CPD) runs all of this through its e-permits system. The rules below come from CPD's Quick Permits and Homeowner Permits pages, checked November 17, 2026. They are the parts most bathroom projects run into; your contractor or CPD has the final word on your specific job.

What is a Denver quick permit, and does a bathroom qualify?

Denver describes quick permits as trade-specific permits issued without plan review. For single-family homes and duplexes, CPD's list includes the trades a bathroom job usually touches:

What does not fit: CPD says most projects cannot be permitted as quick permits, including structural work. Taking out a wall to enlarge a bathroom, adding a bathroom, or reframing a floor for a curbless shower usually means a building permit with plan review, applied for as a building log in e-permits. Quick permits for exterior work on landmarks or in historic districts also cannot be applied for online.

One practical tip: a "simple" tub-to-shower swap often touches three trades at once: the drain and valve (plumbing), a GFCI outlet or light (electrical) and the exhaust fan (mechanical). Ask each bidder to list every permit they will pull, by trade, on the written quote.

Which permit path fits your bathroom project?

Can you pull your own bathroom permit in Denver?

Yes, with limits that catch a lot of people. Denver's homeowner permits are only for a single-family home (a garage can be included). Townhomes, condos, duplexes and ADUs are excluded. You must be the legal owner and live there, with a photo ID that matches the Assessor's records, and trusts, LLCs and corporations cannot hold a homeowner permit.

Plumbing, electrical and mechanical work require passing a homeowner exam (a state license can substitute for the electrical or plumbing exam). You must live in the home for at least one year after the work is complete. One quirk worth knowing: homeowners applying in their own name cannot use e-permits for quick permits; CPD asks you to email the application with a copy of your photo ID instead.

Does the lead-safe rule apply to a Denver bathroom remodel?

If the house was built before 1978, almost certainly. The EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule applies to paid work in pre-1978 homes, and every firm doing it needs EPA certification, including sole proprietors. The only small-job exemption is for disturbing 6 square feet or less of paint per room inside (20 square feet outside), and demolition of painted surfaces is always covered. Tearing out a tub surround or a plaster wall is demolition, so ask every bidder for the firm's RRP certification number. Denver bathroom permit FAQ

Do I need a permit to replace a tub or shower in Denver?

Usually yes. Showers, tubs and sinks are on Denver's plumbing quick permit list, which means a trade permit issued without plan review. If the job also adds outlets or a fan, those trades need their own quick permits.

What is a quick permit in Denver?

A trade-specific permit that Denver issues without plan review, for items like showers, tubs, toilets, water heaters, GFCI outlets, lighting and exhaust fans. Structural work, such as moving walls, cannot use a quick permit.

Can I pull my own bathroom permit in Denver?

Only for a single-family home you own and live in, with a photo ID matching the Assessor's records. Plumbing, electrical and mechanical work need a homeowner exam, and you must live in the home at least one year after the work.

Do condo or townhome owners get homeowner permits in Denver?

No. Denver limits homeowner permits to single-family homes, so condos, townhomes, duplexes and ADUs need a licensed contractor to pull the permit. Check your HOA's rules too.

Does a bathroom remodel in a pre-1978 Denver house need a lead-safe contractor?

Usually. The EPA rule covers paid work that disturbs more than 6 square feet of paint per room inside a pre-1978 home, and demolition of painted surfaces is always covered. Ask for the firm's EPA RRP certification.
